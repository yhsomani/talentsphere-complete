## API, integration, queue, realtime, and media contracts

Root SSOT §§13 and 25–27 (approximately lines 3177–3462) define a 54-entry service contract register, integration removal drill VER-021, separate transactional and heavy queues, and detailed realtime/media behavior. Current target architecture proposes materially different defaults or leaves the contract as a placeholder.

- **SCI-01…SCI-54:** a 54-row candidate crosswalk now retains each operation, actor, idempotency key, candidate API ID, and residual. It records 33 partial candidates and 21 operations with no specific target catalog match. Candidate links are not approved equivalence; source SCI-09…16 (institutional licensing, import, seat operations, media authoring and progress), SCI-19…23 (course versions, review, assignments and peer review), SCI-25 (migration abort/rollback), SCI-27 (payout), SCI-34 (newsletter), SCI-38…39 (assessment/recording), and SCI-45…54 (emerging skills, relationship validation, introductions, feedback, freshness, referrals and alumni) retain material gaps. Names or domain similarity are not coverage evidence.
- **Integration removal:** source VER-021 gates every integration-touching release on a removal drill; target proposes semiannual game days. Decide whether the per-release requirement, target cadence, or a revised risk-tiered policy governs.
- **Queues:** source separates Postgres transactional work (enqueue under 10 ms; process under 5 s; 14 listed job kinds) from heavy durable jobs (9 listed kinds). Target proposes one Postgres fenced-worker engine. Reconcile workload isolation, retry/DLQ, latency, and failure behavior before API/backend contract freeze.
- **Realtime:** source specifies authenticated/RLS-projected channels, payloads ≤4 KB, monotonic cursors, 50 events/s/channel and overflow refresh; launch capacity target is ≤2,500 concurrent connections/40 topics. Target SSE/15-second polling approach does not yet map these contracts or capacities.
- **Media:** source defines MediaSource/adapter/player, five delivery modes, 15 provider capabilities, and AV/transcode/HLS/thumbnail/caption/CDN processing and security. Target `MediaPort` is only a placeholder. Preserve the detailed source scope until an owner explicitly narrows or replaces it.
- **Integrations:** source proposes Supabase substrate, Resend/Postmark, named OAuth, heuristic-first then LLM AI, user-authorized provider delegation, observability, and later HRIS/LMS/media connections. Target proposes PostgreSQL, Brevo, generic identity provider and API-key AI. These are target choices to review, not source facts.

## Security, privacy, and AI controls

Root SSOT sections 15-17 and 31-32 (approximately lines 3591-3885) differ from target security/privacy/backend/assessment contracts in the following decision-relevant areas:

- **Remediation:** source critical <24 h with patch ≤72 h; high <72 h or a seven-day path with blocking controls; medium <14 days. Target allows high up to 14 days and medium up to 60 days. Set severity-specific owner-approved SLAs.
- **AI redaction and evidence:** source redaction covers full names and street addresses and includes company-exclusion/display-restoration behavior. Source also calls for Unicode cleanup, canary-leakage checks, capability-specific suppression/display, evidence/assumption provenance, and evaluation gates. Target coverage is narrower or partial. Do not treat generic PII redaction as equivalent.
- **Provider access:** source distinguishes connected provider/API rights and an order of local processing, free quota, explicitly authorized provider, permitted cloud, then non-AI fallback. Target does not fully carry those distinctions. Resolve under OPEN-24 and OPEN-48; no free-tier cost entitlement is implied.
- **Assessment AI:** source includes four modes, including `POST_ASSESSMENT_ONLY`; target feature spec has two modes, data model three, and backend policy restrictions. Align the lifecycle contract before implementing or claiming assessment AI.
- **Residency and erasure:** source fixes US-East and describes a Phase 3 EU partition, EU AI egress/fallback, and transfer controls; target leaves region undecided. Source FERPA graduation severance is more specific than target generic erasure. Resolve region under OPEN-08/30 and preserve a distinct graduation disposition.
- **RLS:** source says every table is RLS-protected; target physical design exempts internal tables. Reconcile service-role access, tenant isolation, migration policy, and canary coverage. Avoid the broad `RLS everywhere` claim while exemptions remain.
- **Compliance:** source includes explicit commitments; target leaves counsel confirmation open. Treat all applicability statements as provisional until legal review.

## Reliability, release, and service operations

Root SSOT sections 20-22 (approximately lines 3947-4172) carry controls not yet reconciled in target operations documents:

- Source operational response thresholds include P0 acknowledgement ≤10 minutes and P1 ≤30 minutes. Target incident objectives are not fully mapped to these thresholds or to coverage ownership.
- Source performance targets include list/search latency below 200/300 ms and a ≤200 KB bundle budget, while target lists 350/400 ms and route bundles of 220–400 KB. Measure and choose budgets; do not report both as the same target.
- Source rollout uses 5/25/50% stages, defined bake windows, and rollback within 15 seconds; target uses statistical canary opens and remote flag propagation that can take up to 300 seconds. Map detection and rollback consequences explicitly.
- Source database recovery targets are RPO 15 minutes/RTO 2 hours, with quarterly drills; target proposes PITR RPO 5 minutes/RTO 1 hour. Object-storage and regional recovery remain unresolved. Track the source control IDs and selected targets under OPEN-23/49.
- Source requires an integration removal drill for each relevant release; target cadence differs as described above. Preserve evidence requirements when resolving cadence.

## Legacy source evidence conflicts

These findings are conflicts among dated repository documents or between documentation and observed code. They are not future product requirements unless separately adopted.

- `docs/engineering/TARGET_ARCHITECTURE.md` labels several modules, including messaging, `ephemeral` and expected-empty, while `docs/engineering/DATABASE.md` documents PostgreSQL-backed threads/messages and source route inventories label thread/message routes persisted. OPEN-17 still determines whether a production database exists or may be inspected.

The mapping now requires authoritative verification and trustworthy delivery provenance; timestamp-only records remain lower-rung historical claims.

Current `apps/api/src/billing-stripe.ts` contains full-current-period refund handling, and `tests/integration/stripe-billing.test.ts` contains corresponding cases. The legacy prose is stale relative to current source code; it must remain dated evidence, not be treated as the target billing policy or current implementation description.
- `docs/system-model/04-conditions.md` CB-04 says any signed-in account may record a market signal, while `docs/system-model/06-route-authorization.md` records the tested platform-admin-only fix. Current `apps/api/src/server.ts` calls `assertPlatformAdmin` on the route; `tests/integration/skill-forecasting.test.ts` covers the platform-admin path. Keep the earlier condition as historical audit evidence and treat the later fix/code as stronger current evidence.
- Two dated GitHub issue reconciliations assign different subjects to issue numbers #45–49: `docs/reports/GIT_RECONCILIATION_REPORT.md` §4 maps #45 reputation, #46 sandbox, #47 legal, #48 erasure, #49 durability; `docs/reports/REPOSITORY_AUDIT_AND_GITHUB_ISSUE_REPORT.md` §§J–K maps #45 erasure, #46 hosted schema, #47 staging, #48 durability, #49 sandbox. - Market-signal and refund findings illustrate why `docs/system-model/04-conditions.md` and security/billing audit prose cannot be promoted to current behavior without checking later audit notes, handlers, and existing tests. No tests were executed during this documentation pass.
